# PART 1 — STRATEGIC FOUNDATION

## 1. Brand Positioning

**What AXIONWEAR stands for.** AXIONWEAR is Bangladesh's original fandom streetwear label: bold, technical, unapologetically anime- and gaming-inspired graphic tees for fans who want to wear their passion boldly — built entirely on original artwork, never on stolen character art or franchise IP.

**What makes it different.** The current market gives fans two bad options: cheap Facebook-page bootleg tees that lift character art directly from franchises (thin fabric, blurry prints, real legal risk to buyer and seller), or official licensed merch that's expensive, hard to access, and limited to whichever franchise happens to have a licensing deal. AXIONWEAR is the third option — **premium quality, 100% original designs that capture the actual feeling of shonen anime, mecha tech, and gaming culture (the power-up, the final battle, the blade drawn at the right moment, the pride of being exactly the kind of fan you are) without depicting a single copyrighted character.**

**Position in the Bangladesh apparel market.** Above bootleg anime-print sellers on originality, legality, and construction quality. Below/parallel to imported official licensed merchandise on price and local accessibility, while offering something official merch structurally can't: broad fandom-energy designs not locked to one specific franchise's licensing deal.

**Long-term positioning beyond the first collection.** Start as a 100-unit, 4-design capsule that proves the visual language and the quality bar. Long-term, become the label Bangladesh's anime/gaming community trusts by default for original, premium fandom apparel — eventually expanding into more categories and, once credibility and scale exist, potentially legitimate licensed collaborations.

# PART 2 — VISUAL IDENTITY

## 8. Visual Identity Strategy

Core symbol is the **X-mark** (locked). The aesthetic is **tech / mecha / gaming / glitch** — sharp, precise, futuristic, never soft or vintage-streetwear. The palette is monochrome-forward (black, white, charcoal, metallic silver) with one accent color, still to be locked (Section 11 gives a specific recommendation). Every asset must reproduce cleanly through the brand's real production constraints: single- or two-color screen printing and DTG at small Dhaka vendors, woven labels at small scale, and embroidery — meaning no gradients, no fine glitch detail in the core garment print, and a design that still reads correctly in one flat color.

## 9. Logo System

**Primary Logo — Wordmark + X-mark combined lockup.** "AXIONWEAR" set in Chakra Petch Bold (see Typography, Section 12) paired directly with the X-mark, per the locked identity brief. Used on the website header, packaging, and any full-signature application.

**Wordmark alone — "AXIONWEAR."** Used where the full name needs to appear without the mark — invoices, email footers, secondary packaging panels.

**Monogram / Symbol — the X-mark.** Two angular, faceted strokes forming an X — beveled, cut-corner geometry (echoing Chakra Petch's own cut-corner letterforms) rather than a soft rounded X. A small deliberate gap at the center — an "ignition point" — reads as the mark's own hidden energy source, tying the symbol directly back to the brand name. This avoids the two generic traps explicitly flagged in the brief: it isn't a literal crossed-swords/katana cliché, and it isn't a generic esports-crest X. It survives single-color screen print and small-scale embroidery.

**Secondary Logo — Compact Lockup.** X-mark plus "AXION" (dropping "WEAR") for smaller applications — sleeve tags, small packaging panels, social highlight covers.

**Icon.** The X-mark alone — Instagram/Facebook profile picture, website favicon, zipper-pull, embroidered sleeve or hem mark.

**Branding hierarchy on garments:** see Section 18 — the X-mark stays secondary on the garment itself; the collection artwork (Final Hour, Shonen Power-Up, Blade Line, Otaku Pride) is the primary visual moment on each shirt.

## 10. Logo Design Principles

The system communicates **Precision + Movement + Confidence + Modernity** through: faceted, beveled strokes (precision/tech), the diagonal ignition-point gap (movement/energy), bold weight and confident scale (confidence), and a restrained glitch-offset treatment reserved for digital-only executions (modernity, used sparingly).

**Explicitly avoided:** literal crossed-katana or crossed-sword clichés, generic esports-team X-crests, copied character silhouettes, stock circuit-board clipart, overused chrome/gradient effects on the core logo file (chrome/metallic finishes are reserved for specific hero applications only, never the base logo asset), and anything that could be mistaken for existing franchise or brand IP.

## 11. Color System

**Core palette (locked direction: monochrome-forward, one accent still to be finalized)**

| Name | HEX | RGB | CMYK | Pantone (reference only) | Primary use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AXION Black | #0A0A0A | 10, 10, 10 | 0, 0, 0, 96 | Pantone Black 6 C | Garments (batch 1 base color), logo, packaging, website |
| AXION White | #FFFFFF | 255, 255, 255 | 0, 0, 0, 0 | Pantone White | Print ink on black garments, logo reversal, backgrounds |
| Charcoal | #2B2B2B | 43, 43, 43 | 0, 0, 0, 83 | ≈ Pantone Black 7 C | Secondary UI/packaging tone, tonal print option |
| Metallic Silver | #C4C4C4 | 196, 196, 196 | 0, 0, 0, 23 | ≈ Pantone Cool Gray 4 C | Secondary print ink, thread color, hardware/foil accents |

**Recommended accent color: Ion Cyan — #00E5FF** (RGB 0, 229, 255 · CMYK ≈ 100, 10, 0, 0 · Pantone ≈ Process Cyan C). This is a recommendation for Farhad to lock, not yet a final decision. Reasoning: cyan reads immediately as "energy/charge" — it's the default color of HUD interfaces, mecha visor glow, and glitch/scan-line effects across the exact genres AXIONWEAR draws from, and it ties literally back to "ion" in the brand name. Teal was the other option explored; cyan is the stronger pick specifically because it's more saturated and electric, which matches "sharp, precise, futuristic" better than teal's calmer, more muted register. If Farhad wants a warmer or more BD-specific alternative, a secondary option worth testing is a magenta/cyan glitch-duo (common in genuine glitch aesthetics) — but that adds production cost and should only be considered once cyan is validated.

**Usage discipline:** the accent is a spot color, not a base garment color. For batch 1 specifically, garments stay black-only (per the locked production rule) — the accent shows up only in secondary touches: a thread highlight on the woven label, an interior packaging color, a digital-only UI/social treatment, or a single accent line within a print where the vendor's screen-printing setup allows a second color at reasonable cost. This keeps the accent special rather than diluted, and keeps batch-1 print costs realistic for a small Dhaka run.

**Monochrome + accent rule:** the X-mark and wordmark must always work in pure black, pure white, and black-on-white/white-on-black reversal with zero dependency on the accent color. The accent is a highlight, never a requirement for recognition.

## 12. Typography System

**Primary Typeface — Chakra Petch (Google Fonts, free).** Angular, cut-corner geometric sans that reads as technical/sci-fi natively — the closest free, commercially unrestricted match to the "bold, geometric, aggressive" brief. Used for the logo lockup, campaign headlines, and section headers. Bold/SemiBold weights, always uppercase, wide tracking on large headline use.

**Secondary Typeface — Rajdhani (Google Fonts, free).** A condensed, technical companion face used for subheads, stat-card labels, and HUD-style UI text (size grids, "power stat" blocks on hang tags — see Section 17). SemiBold/Medium weights, uppercase.

**Body Typeface — Inter (Google Fonts, free).** Used for product descriptions, care instructions, and general website/app body copy, where Chakra Petch's stylization would hurt legibility at small sizes. Regular/Medium weight, sentence case.

**Optional Display Typeface — Orbitron.** Reserved narrowly for large campaign numerals or "power level" stat displays. Flagged deliberately: Orbitron is a very recognizable "sci-fi/gamer" font and risks feeling generic if overused — restrict it to large-scale numeral/stat moments only, never body text or the core logo.

**Hierarchy**

1. Logo / campaign titles — Chakra Petch Bold, uppercase, wide tracking
2. Collection/section titles, stat labels — Rajdhani SemiBold, uppercase
3. Product names / SKU codes — Rajdhani Medium, uppercase
4. Body copy / descriptions — Inter Regular, sentence case
5. Fine print / care info — Inter Regular, reduced size, Charcoal or Silver color
6. Campaign hero numerals (selective) — Orbitron Bold, uppercase, large scale only

## 13. Graphic Language

The recognizable system beyond the logo draws directly from mecha-HUD and gaming-genre conventions, generic enough to never reference a specific franchise's actual trademarked UI: **HUD corner brackets** framing photography and product shots, **circuit-line traces** as border/divider elements, **glitch-offset duplication** (a light RGB-split echo of an edge) used only in digital/campaign executions, **energy/power-gauge bars** as a graphic motif (an original take on the universal "power meter" convention, not copied from any one game), **scan-line texture overlays** on campaign imagery, and **single-stroke blade linework** as a recurring graphic device (a generic weapon silhouette, never a specific franchise's weapon).

A gamified rarity-tier system is used for SKU/collection labeling — e.g., `AX-01 // STANDARD DROP` — a broad, ownable genre convention (rarity tiers exist across countless games generically) rather than a copy of any single title's specific rarity-color system. This shows up on hang tags, secondary labels, and social product-reveal graphics — recognizable as AXIONWEAR even without the logo present.

## 14. Photography Direction

**Lighting.** Dramatic, single-source or colored rim lighting — using the Ion Cyan accent as a signature rim-light or gel color is the brand's photographic signature move, distinct from flat even lighting.

**Background.** Studio: black or deep charcoal seamless for product shots. Campaign: urban Dhaka at night or in low-light — streets, rooftops, underpasses — chosen for existing shadow and contrast, not built sets.

**Composition / angles.** Dynamic, implied-motion poses and slightly low or dramatic angles — the opposite of static stillness; energy and movement are the point.

**Model direction / expression.** Confident, intense, "in the moment" — captured mid-motion or in a charged, ready stance rather than a passive standing pose.

**Clothing presentation.** True fit, full-length and detail shots; print/graphic detail gets close-up coverage to show line quality and ink precision.

**Color treatment.** High contrast, deep true blacks, controlled use of the cyan accent as a rim-light or environmental glow — never a full color-wash filter across the whole image.

**Product photography vs. campaign photography.** Product photography stays clean, consistent, and well-lit for e-commerce comparison (front/back/detail/on-body, same background and angle set every time). Campaign photography is where the dramatic lighting, motion, and night-time Dhaka settings live — building the world the graphics come from, not selling one SKU directly.

## 15. Packaging System

Keeping this realistic for a bootstrapped, small-batch operation:

**Default order packaging.** Black-printed poly mailer bag (low-cost, standard for small BD apparel sellers), single-color X-mark print on the exterior — this is what every regular order ships in.

**Launch-drop "hero" packaging (limited run, content/shoot use).** A small run of black kraft mailer boxes, X-mark stamped or single-color printed on top, reserved for the initial 100-unit drop specifically — worth the extra cost once, for launch unboxing content and first-buyer experience, not as an ongoing standard.

**Sticker.** Die-cut X-mark, small holographic-foil variant included with select orders — cheap to produce, high appeal to a sticker-collecting fandom audience, doubles as low-cost marketing that travels on laptops/water bottles/notebooks.

**Thank-you card / insert card.** Small black card, white/silver text, gamer-coded tone — e.g., *"Loadout complete. Thanks for gearing up with us."*

**Care card.** Styled like a stat card: fabric composition and GSM, wash instructions, "Class: Tee," "Collection: 01 — Ignition," SKU code — technical-transparency framing the audience will recognize and enjoy rather than a plain care label.

## 16. Woven Label System

**Main neck label.** Black woven ground, white or silver thread. X-mark centered above, "AXIONWEAR" wordmark below, size grid at the base. ~5cm x 3cm, satin weave.

**Secondary label.** "Made in Bangladesh," collection code (`AX-01`), product code — off-white or black twill, single-thread-color print/weave for legibility at small scale.

**Side / hem label.** X-mark alone, small tab — a quiet secondary touch.

## 17. Hang Tag System

**Front.** X-mark, centered, black or charcoal card stock.

**Back.** Product name and SKU code, a playful "stat block" (e.g., *Durability: fabric weight in GSM · Mobility: fit description · Class: Tee · Collection: 01 — Ignition*), QR code to the product page, social handles, website.

**Specifications.** ~5cm x 8cm, matte card stock (250–300gsm), single hole top-left, black cord loop. Silver foil or single-color print on the X-mark for select launch-drop tags as a small premium touch, budget permitting.

## 18. Apparel Application

**Branding hierarchy.** `AXIONWEAR` (full lockup) is reserved for the neck label, hang tag, and packaging. `AXION` or the `X-mark` alone is the everyday garment mark — sleeve tag, hem, small chest placement. The **collection artwork itself** (The Final Hour / Shonen Power-Up / Blade Line / Otaku Pride) is the primary visual moment on the shirt — front or back, large-scale — with the X-mark kept small and secondary (sleeve or chest) so it never competes with the hero graphic. This is a deliberate difference from generic streetwear logo-tees: the fandom artwork sells the shirt, the X-mark quietly signs it.

**Rule of thumb:** one hero graphic placement per shirt, one small X-mark placement, nothing else — keeps each design legible and keeps unit screen-print costs realistic.

## 27. Competitive Positioning

- **Bootleg anime-print Facebook sellers:** AXIONWEAR wins on originality, legal safety, and construction quality — the segment this brand is most directly built to outclass.
- **Official licensed merch (imported):** AXIONWEAR wins on price and local access, while offering something structurally different — broad fandom-genre energy, not locked to one franchise's licensing deal.
- **Generic Bangladeshi streetwear:** AXIONWEAR wins by speaking fandom language directly instead of generic slogans or trend-chasing graphics.
- **International anime/gaming collab drops (e.g., mainstream retailer x-anime collabs):** AXIONWEAR is hyper-local, small-batch, and community-specific — collectible in a way a global mass-market collab can't be.

**Where AXIONWEAR sits on price × quality × identity:** priced above bootleg sellers, below official imported licensed merch; highest in the local market on originality and legal integrity; visual identity discipline (X-mark system, graphic language, monochrome+accent palette) exceeds what any current bootleg or generic local competitor is running.

## 28. Brand Differentiation

1. **100% original, IP-safe artwork.** Bootleg competitors carry real legal and quality risk; AXIONWEAR's designs are built from scratch to capture genre energy without depicting protected characters — a defensible, verifiable difference, not a marketing claim.
2. **Local fandom fluency.** Designed by and for Bangladesh's fandom community, referencing shared fan experience directly, rather than generic global streetwear tropes.
3. **Numbered, weighted small-batch drops.** A publicly stated 40/22/22/16 split across 100 units creates genuine, gamified scarcity and "which design did you get" collectibility — not a vague "limited edition" claim.
4. **Stat-card technical transparency.** Care cards and hang tags present fabric, fit, and construction details in a format the gaming-literate audience already recognizes and enjoys, building trust through format as much as content.
5. **Recognizable without borrowed characters.** The X-mark plus the graphic language stays recognizable on its own — where bootleg competitors depend entirely on featuring the (stolen) character art itself to sell the shirt.
6. **Small-team, community-responsive model.** A lean FB/IG-native operation can react to real fandom trends and community feedback far faster than slow official licensors or anonymous mass-market bootleg sellers.

## 29. Brand Scalability

The X-mark, the monochrome-plus-accent palette, and the energy/gaming naming lexicon are category-agnostic — they extend cleanly into hoodies, caps, bags, and further accessories by extending the SKU-coding pattern (`AX-[Collection]-[Category]-[Design]`), without touching the core logo, color, or typography system. Future limited collaborations can introduce a second accent color scoped only to that collaboration, the same way the current accent is scoped to the core brand. Once AXIONWEAR has scale and credibility, the same identity system is built to support legitimate licensed collaborations without needing a rebrand.
